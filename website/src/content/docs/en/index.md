mailWarmer keeps your inboxes active by subscribing them to real newsletters. There is no account to create, no server, and nothing to configure. This page walks through the whole flow once, start to finish. Each tab also has its own page in the menu.

## 1. Install the extension

1. Open [mailWarmer on the Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mail-warmer/ikibedkcbfcilgignjobgdlioninmige).
2. Click **Add to Chrome**, then confirm with **Add extension**.

It works in Chrome, Edge, Brave and other Chromium-based browsers on desktop.

## 2. Pin the icon and open the side panel

1. Click the puzzle-piece icon to the right of the address bar.
2. Click the pin next to **mailWarmer**, so the icon stays in the toolbar.
3. Click the mailWarmer icon. The side panel opens on the right side of the window.

The panel follows your browser's language: English or Português (Brasil). Hover any tab for a one-line hint about what it is for.

## 3. Add the inboxes you want to keep warm

<figure class="shot"><img src="/images/docs/en/emails.webp" alt="The Emails tab with three addresses added" width="400" height="760" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Step 1 · the Emails tab</figcaption></figure>

Open the **Emails** tab — it is step 1.

1. Type the address in the first field.
2. Optionally give it a label, such as *Main* or *Side project*, so you can tell your inboxes apart.
3. Click **Add**.

Repeat for every address you want to keep active. There is no limit. Only add addresses you own or are allowed to use.

## 4. Pick newsletters and run

<figure class="shot"><img src="/images/docs/en/run.webp" alt="The Run tab with newsletters, statuses and the From selector" width="400" height="760" loading="lazy" /><figcaption>Step 2 · the Run tab</figcaption></figure>

Open the **Run** tab — step 2.

1. In **From**, choose the address to subscribe.
2. Tick the newsletters you want. **Select all** ticks every one; **Un-subscribed only** ticks just the ones this address isn't subscribed to yet.
3. Click **Run signups**, the big button at the top.

mailWarmer goes through your selection one newsletter at a time, with a pause of a few seconds between each, like a person signing up by hand. You can keep browsing while it works: form signups happen in background tabs that close by themselves. Leave Chrome open until the run finishes.

While it runs, the status at the top right says **Running** and the toolbar icon counts successful signups in orange.

## 5. Check the results — and confirm

Every row ends the run with a status: <span class="pill ok">success</span> or <span class="pill err">error</span>. The toolbar badge turns green with the number of successes. The **Log** tab has a line for every signup, with the reason when one fails.

Then open the inbox you just warmed up.

> **Confirm your subscriptions.** Many newsletters send a "Confirm your subscription" email first. mailWarmer never opens your inbox, so click those confirmation links yourself — a newsletter that is never confirmed never starts sending. Check the Promotions and Spam folders too.

Within a day or two the newsletters start arriving on their own schedule, and the inbox reads as an active account.

## 6. Optional extras

None of these are required. Use them when they help:

- **[Cookies](/docs/cookies)** — warm the browser up before signups, so it looks lived-in.
- **[Profile](/docs/profile)** — the name, city and other details used when a form asks for more than an email.
- **[Lists](/docs/lists)** — see the curated list, add your own newsletters, or fix a form that fails.

## Your data stays with you

mailWarmer has no server and sends nothing to us: no statistics, no tracking, no install ID. Your addresses, profile and history live in your browser's local extension storage. The only requests it makes are the signups and warm-up visits you start. Details are in the [Privacy Policy](/privacy).
